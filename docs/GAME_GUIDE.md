# ZKSCAPE — Game Guide

> **Spoiler policy:** this guide explains how the game *works*. It does not describe what
> happens in the ending cinematic, who turns out to be behind anything, or the final reveal.
> That is yours to find.

---

## 1. Before you start

ZKSCAPE opens on a landing screen.

1. **Look up your zkSNARK.** Enter the number of the NFT you hold (1–10,000). That piece
   becomes your explorer and is drawn in every scene. This is the only way in — there is no
   upload option, so a valid zkSNARK number is required to play.
2. Press **YOUR WORLD IS READY. ENTER ZKSCAPE**.
3. The intro cinematic plays. It runs to its natural end — there is no skip.
4. You arrive in the **Hub**.

You may be asked for an explorer name the first time you reach the hub. It is optional. If you
give one, it floats above your character in a pixel font. You can change or remove it later from
the profile chip in the top-right of the hub.

## 2. Controls

### Keyboard

| Action | Keys |
|---|---|
| Walk left | `←` / `A` |
| Walk right | `→` / `D` |
| Jump | `Space` / `↑` / `W` |
| Interact, talk, inspect, use | `E` / `Enter` / `F` |
| Close a panel | `Esc` |

Worlds can add their own keys. Whenever a world needs one, it shows a prompt near the relevant
object naming the key — for example a shield toggle, or menu navigation in the ZK Market.

### Touch

On a phone or tablet an on-screen pad appears at the bottom of every world:

- **bottom-left:** `◀` and `▶`
- **bottom-right:** an interact/talk button and a jump button

In the hub, you can also **tap anywhere on the ground** and your explorer will walk to that spot.

### Reading prompts

When you are close to something you can act on, a label appears over it:

- `[E] TALK` on desktop, `TAP ✋ TALK` on touch — an NPC.
- `[E] INSPECT` — a discovery.
- `[E] WAKE THE CORE` — a story beat.
- On a HUD-less world, the first few seconds show `← → WALK SPACE JUMP` as a one-time reminder.

---

## 3. The Hub

The hub is a side-scrolling world of its own. Your explorer starts near a portal and can walk
left and right along it.

- **Portals** sit along the ground as landmarks. Walk to one and press interact to enter that
  world.
- **Sealed worlds** tell you what unlocks them: usually the previous world, or The Origin for
  the optional ones.
- The terrain changes as you progress. New ground opens up.
- **Discoveries counter** in the top-left shows how many core worlds you have completed.
- **🏆 LEADERBOARD** (top-right) opens the optional online rankings.
- **Profile chip** (top-right) lets you change your explorer name.

Two worlds sit up on a terrace at the far right of the hub. Those are the **optional worlds**.

---

## 4. Exploration and interaction

Every world follows the same loop:

1. **Walk.** Movement is momentum-based with acceleration, and jumping has coyote time and a
   jump buffer, so it feels forgiving.
2. **Approach.** When you are in range of something meaningful, a prompt appears.
3. **Interact** with `E` / the talk button. Dialogue types itself out; press interact again to
   advance, and again to move on.
4. **Solve.** Some discoveries open a puzzle panel directly on the world canvas. Solve it to
   claim the find.
5. **Carry it back.** Many finds must be handed to a specific NPC. The walk back matters —
   the NPC will comment on how you found it and on what you did along the way.

### Talking to people

NPCs are not quest markers. They open up as you explore, they remember what you have done, and
some of them will tell you things that only make sense later. Talking is never required to
finish, but skipping everything leaves a lot of the story unsaid.

### Puzzles

Puzzles are drawn in-place, not in a separate menu, and are always solvable by looking at the
world itself — switch states, read the writing, count things, follow the geometry. If a puzzle
seems stuck, walk away and come back; several reveal themselves only from a specific angle or
after you have seen something earlier.

## 5. Progress and unlocking

- Worlds unlock in order. The tenth, The Archive, only opens once you have discovered the first
  nine.
- **The Origin** additionally unlocks the two optional worlds.
- Your progress is saved continuously in this browser. You can leave and come back.

---

## 6. The two optional worlds

Once The Origin is discovered, a terrace opens at the far right of the hub with two more
worlds on it:

- **THE NOIR GATE** — a private wallet city.
- **THE ZK MARKET** — shielded identities, on display.

Neither is required to complete the core ten. Both are opt-in detours, and both have their own
rules about what you are and are not allowed to do with what you find. They also let you export
something at the end.

They are worth playing even if you have already finished the main ten.

---

## 7. The Archive and the final trial

**The Archive** is the tenth and final core world. Unlike the others it does not hand you a
shard — it gives you a **final trial**: a ten-question set drawn from the whole Zcash story you
have just walked through.

The set is built deliberately:

- **15 questions** — 2 easy, 5 medium, 8 hard
- drawn from categories such as Origins, Privacy, Proofs, Keys, Protocol, Upgrades, Notes and Ecosystem
- the same question never repeats inside one run, and runs differ from each other

**Hints are available but tracked.** Using a hint is allowed; using *zero* hints is worth bonus
points.

### Scoring

Your final score is itemised in-game, and every component is explained on the results screen:

| Component | How it is earned |
|---|---|
| World exploration | 500 per core world discovered |
| Artifacts | 250 per artifact (core + optional) |
| Bonus worlds | 500 per optional world |
| Canvas | 500 for finishing The Canvas |
| Archive correct | 100 per correct answer |
| Streak | 50 per correct answer that directly follows another correct answer |
| No hints | 500 for a clean run |
| Accuracy | 1000 at 100%, 500 at 90%+ |
| Time bonus | 3000 minus 10 per second |

### The clock

The run timer starts when you enter the game and stops when you finish The Archive.

It **pauses automatically** whenever the tab is hidden or unfocused, and resumes when you come
back. Time spent away is not counted, and is not included in anything you submit.

### The Explorer Card

Finishing The Archive produces your **Explorer Card** — a keepsake image drawn from the things
the game already knows about you: your PFP, your palette, your explorer name, your rank, your
discovery count and your statistics.

The card has **five tiers**, chosen by your score-based rank:

| Rank | Card |
|---|---|
| Rookie | Iron Tablet |
| Explorer | Bronze Compass |
| Pathfinder | Silver Octagon |
| Guardian | Crimson Bastion |
| Architect | Prismatic Relic |

Press **DOWNLOAD EXPLORER CARD** on the results screen to save it as a PNG.

The card is generated entirely in your browser. You can download it whether or not the
leaderboard is configured or reachable.

### After the ending

The closing cinematic plays when the run completes. It runs to its end.

## 8. Optional leaderboard

Entirely opt-in, and only available after you have completed the Archive.

Press the leaderboard button on the Explorer Card screen, choose a display name (3–16
characters; no email, wallet or real name needed) and submit. If you would rather not, skip
it — nothing is sent and your Explorer Card is unaffected.

> **Note:** the hub's leaderboard button has been removed for now, so the leaderboard is
> currently only reachable from the Explorer Card screen after finishing a run.

What is sent, and what is not, is documented in [PRIVACY.md](PRIVACY.md).

## 9. Frequently asked

**Can I skip the intro cinematic?**
No. It plays to its natural end.

**I lost my progress.**
Progress is per-browser. If you are in a private window, or your browser clears storage on
exit, it will not persist. Use **RESET PROGRESS** on the landing screen to start over
deliberately.

**Do I have to talk to the NPCs?**
No, but the story is mostly in them.

**Do I have to play the optional worlds?**
No. They are optional by design.

**Is the Explorer Card an NFT or on-chain?**
No. It is an in-browser collectible image, generated locally.
